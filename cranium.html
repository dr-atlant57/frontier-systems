Human Systems Division / Frontier Systems

CRANIUM

A structural approach to the head and neck — designed around the idea that the upper system is not a fragment, but a primary architectural layer of the human system.

01 / POSITION

Start with the primary structure.

CRANIUM studies the relationship between the cranio-cervical region, structural axes, vectors and the compensatory patterns that emerge through the body.

The objective is not to isolate a symptom. It is to understand the architecture of the system and work from its upper structural layer.

02 / PRINCIPLES

Structure

Observe geometry, axes, vectors and relationships before interpreting isolated signals.

Sequence

Work from primary structural relationships toward secondary compensations.

Systems

Evaluate the human body as an interconnected dynamic architecture rather than disconnected parts.

03 / FRAMEWORK

Human architecture as a system.

CRANIUM is the first Human Systems project within Frontier Systems Architecture Bureau. The same architectural logic used for complex organizations and intelligent systems is applied here to the human structural layer: reality, structure, dependencies, intervention, measurement.

04 / NEXT

Architecture before optimization.

This is a developing research and practice platform. Its next layer is the CRANIUM protocol, measurement framework and system map.
