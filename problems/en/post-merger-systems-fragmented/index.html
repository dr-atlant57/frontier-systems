Post-merger systems are fragmented: where synergies disappear

Companies merged on paper. Their operations did not.

After an acquisition, purchasing, reporting, customer records and decision rights often remain separated. Replacing every system may cost too much; leaving all systems isolated may prevent promised synergies. The useful question is which connections actually create measurable value.

Symptoms

  • Group reporting requires spreadsheet reconciliation and arrives late.
  • Shared customers, suppliers and idle resources stay invisible.
  • Cross-company meetings multiply without a clear process owner.

Potential root causes

  • Master data definitions and process boundaries disagree.
  • There is no common model of authority or performance.
  • Synergy assumptions were never translated into testable operating changes.

Questions for the audit

  • Which promised synergy is measurable within one quarter?
  • Which operations must remain legally or technically independent?
  • Who can authorize cross-company process changes?

Evidence to measure

  • Time to consolidated reporting
  • Verified cost or cycle-time reduction
  • Unresolved integration exceptions
