RESEARCH DOSSIER · REQUIRES VALIDATION

Vendor lock-in stops enterprise transformation before it starts

A critical business process should not be trapped inside one vendor's roadmap.

An organization depends on a legacy product for contracts, reporting or operations. New capabilities demand expensive custom integrations, while migration threatens continuity. Replacing everything may introduce more risk than it removes; doing nothing can entrench dependence.

Symptoms

  • A new workflow requires long supplier change requests and expensive customization.
  • Exports exist but business meanings and rules are not portable.
  • Management delays innovation because a core migration feels all-or-nothing.

Potential root causes

  • Business rules are embedded in a proprietary implementation.
  • Data and process contracts are undocumented.
  • Migration success is defined as cutover, not sustained correctness.
01 / ARCHITECTURE

Alternative architectures

01

Strangler migration

Gradually replace selected features behind stable interfaces.

02

Semantic adapter layer

Separate business entities and permission rules from proprietary formats.

03

Dual-run verification

Compare legacy and new outcomes on the same authorized cases before cutover.

Questions for the audit

  • Which critical business rule exists only inside the vendor system?
  • What functionality can be decoupled without changing the system of record?
  • How will output equivalence and rollback be demonstrated?

Evidence to measure

  • Time and cost to introduce a new workflow
  • Share of rules documented independently
  • Mismatch rate during parallel runs
02 / DISCOVERY

Proposed first deliverable

A staged decoupling architecture and reversible migration experiment.
