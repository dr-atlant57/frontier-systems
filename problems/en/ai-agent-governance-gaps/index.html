RESEARCH DOSSIER · REQUIRES VALIDATION

Who controls autonomous AI agents in the enterprise?

Autonomy without accountability is not a production architecture.

AI agents can call tools, edit records and initiate transactions. If permitted actions and evidence trails are undefined, speed gains can introduce a new source of operational failure. Governance must be embedded in architecture, not added after rollout.

Symptoms

  • Agents act on business systems without consistent approval rules.
  • Teams cannot reconstruct why an action occurred.
  • Errors repeat across linked agent workflows without escalation.

Potential root causes

  • Tools were connected before authority and threats were modeled.
  • Monitoring measures tokens but not harmful actions or accepted outcomes.
  • Rollback, uncertainty and human escalation have no defined owner.
01 / ARCHITECTURE

Alternative architectures

01

Risk-tiered permissions

Categorize actions by reversibility, impact and approval requirement.

02

Agent control plane

Unify identity, policy enforcement, audit trails and independent tests.

03

Staged bounded autonomy

Begin read-only, then supervised recommendations, then approved actions.

Questions for the audit

  • Which actions can alter money, access or external obligations?
  • Can every agent action be reconstructed and attributed?
  • How will incorrect actions be stopped and reversed?

Evidence to measure

  • Auditable actions as share of total
  • Blocked unauthorized actions
  • Time to detect and resolve incidents
02 / DISCOVERY

Proposed first deliverable

A risk-tiered agent architecture with testable controls and escalation.
