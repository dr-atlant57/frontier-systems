RESEARCH DOSSIER · REQUIRES VALIDATION

Suppliers are available but delivery capacity remains invisible

Finding a contractor is not the same as managing delivery.

A project coordinator can identify dozens of qualified suppliers yet still struggle to know their current capacity, actual progress and exception paths. Supplier directories capture what providers claim to do, not verified delivery readiness. Each additional company introduces its own task vocabulary and reporting cycle.

Symptoms

  • Teams repeatedly request availability from the same suppliers.
  • Procurement commits before a delivery dependency has been verified.
  • The customer learns about a missed milestone only after its deadline.

Potential root causes

  • Capabilities, resources and capacity are stored in disconnected formats.
  • Order status is reported manually and does not distinguish evidence from promise.
  • Escalation ownership between customer and contractor is not explicit.
01 / ARCHITECTURE

Alternative architectures

01

Verified supplier capability passports

Separate claimed skills, evidence, geography and actual capacity.

02

Shared delivery-state protocol

Use agreed milestones, proofs, exception types and permissions between organizations.

03

Cross-company coordination agents

Automate permitted status checks while routing changes and disputes to accountable people.

Questions for the audit

  • Which missing supplier signal causes the most delays?
  • How is capacity verified instead of merely stated?
  • Who approves changes to a contractual milestone?

Evidence to measure

  • Time from request to verified supplier match
  • Milestones supported by evidence
  • Delay detection lead time
02 / DISCOVERY

Proposed first deliverable

A supplier-state ontology and one traceable cross-company delivery pilot.
