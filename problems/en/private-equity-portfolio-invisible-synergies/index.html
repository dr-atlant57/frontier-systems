A private equity portfolio has synergies nobody can quantify

Owning several companies does not automatically produce shared value.

A holding group can acquire businesses with overlapping customers, skills, suppliers and infrastructure yet lack a consistent way to identify actual joint opportunities. Consolidated financial results show outcomes, not which complementary capability is idle or usable across entities. Centralization without evidence may damage local advantages.

Symptoms

  • Management presents theoretical portfolio synergies without operational baselines.
  • Portfolio companies separately buy overlapping services and capabilities.
  • Investment committees cannot distinguish attainable coordination gains from speculative savings.

Potential root causes

  • Cross-company assets and capabilities have no shared taxonomy.
  • Financial reporting does not expose process-level dependencies.
  • No experimental framework ranks synergies by effort, consent and verified impact.
01 / ARCHITECTURE

Alternative architectures

01

Portfolio capability graph

Model facilities, assets, customers, skills and constraints with appropriate access rights.

02

Synergy experiment portfolio

Prioritize narrow cross-company tests before permanent consolidation.

03

Shared service coordination layer

Deploy reusable workflows only after local acceptance and measurable benefit.

Questions for the audit

  • Which two portfolio companies have a plausible measurable shared opportunity?
  • Who may exchange customer, supplier and pricing information under law and contract?
  • What experiment proves the gain without mandatory consolidation?

Evidence to measure

  • Verified cross-portfolio savings or revenue
  • Time to test a new cross-company service
  • Local operational performance after coordination
02 / DISCOVERY

Proposed first deliverable

A portfolio opportunity graph and prioritized value-creation experiments.

Method and limitations
