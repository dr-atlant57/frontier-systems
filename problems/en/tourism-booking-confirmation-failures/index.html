RESEARCH DOSSIER · REQUIRES VALIDATION

A tourist has a reservation but the service is not actually ready

An accepted booking is not the same as a verified arrival.

Travelers may hold valid confirmations while accommodation, transport or local providers cannot fulfill the promise. Guests and small operators often rely on scattered chats and unverifiable status messages. A useful platform should separate booking intention, provider acceptance, real readiness, exception handling and authorized alternatives.

Symptoms

  • Guests discover an unavailable room or transfer after arrival.
  • Hosts cannot quickly propose an acceptable substitute with clear terms.
  • Refund and complaint histories disappear across chats and providers.

Potential root causes

  • Confirmation statuses have no shared meaning across participants.
  • Changes are not acknowledged by all affected parties.
  • Responsibility for replacement, compensation and evidence is unclear.
01 / ARCHITECTURE

Alternative architectures

01

Verified service passport

Track provider identity, service conditions, availability and confirmation evidence.

02

Two-sided confirmation protocol

Require clear status changes and time-bound acknowledgements.

03

Assisted service recovery

Offer lawful, consented alternatives and escalate unresolved disputes to humans.

Questions for the audit

  • What does confirmed mean to each provider and guest?
  • Who has authority to accept a substitute or compensation?
  • What connectivity and language limitations exist on location?

Evidence to measure

  • Bookings fulfilled as explicitly confirmed
  • Time to resolve a failed service
  • Share of alternatives accepted with informed consent
02 / DISCOVERY

Proposed first deliverable

A verified booking-state model and one measured multi-party service recovery pilot.
