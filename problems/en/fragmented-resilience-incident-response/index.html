RESEARCH DOSSIER · REQUIRES VALIDATION

A company has backups but cannot demonstrate business recovery

Recovery is a coordinated business process, not a storage checkbox.

Backups and disaster recovery tooling may be in place, yet a failure across suppliers, applications and people can still stop delivery. Restoring one database is not the same as restoring a complete business service and proving its integrity.

Symptoms

  • Technical teams report successful backups but end-to-end recovery has not been rehearsed.
  • Critical service dependencies are distributed across undocumented systems.
  • During incidents, authority for customer communication and recovery priorities is unclear.

Potential root causes

  • Recovery objectives exist at system level, not business-service level.
  • Dependencies and third-party obligations are not mapped.
  • Exercises test isolated restores without business acceptance checks.
01 / ARCHITECTURE

Alternative architectures

01

Business-service dependency map

Trace critical outcomes through systems, people and providers.

02

Recovery choreography

Define ordered technical and organizational recovery with clear decision rights.

03

Controlled failure experiments

Test realistic scenarios and measure restored service acceptance.

Questions for the audit

  • Which customer-facing outcome must recover first?
  • Can the team prove service integrity after a technical restore?
  • Who can authorize failover and communicate to stakeholders?

Evidence to measure

  • Verified end-to-end recovery time
  • Share of critical dependencies exercised
  • Business acceptance failures after restore
02 / DISCOVERY

Proposed first deliverable

A business-service recovery dependency model and one measured exercise.
