RESEARCH DOSSIER · REQUIRES VALIDATION

AI agents from different companies cannot safely coordinate

Two smart agents do not automatically share the same authority or trust.

One organization may deploy a purchasing agent while a supplier uses a separate assistant. Exchanging natural-language messages can create unapproved commitments, reveal commercial information and hide responsibility. Agent-to-agent cooperation needs explicit identities, scopes, approvals and records before it can become an operational channel.

Symptoms

  • Agents propose delivery or price commitments without evidence of authority.
  • Sensitive customer or supplier data is copied into external agent conversations.
  • Participants cannot reconstruct which side approved an automated transaction.

Potential root causes

  • Agent identity is confused with authority to act for an organization.
  • Data-sharing policies are not tied to individual contract tasks.
  • The boundary between suggestion, negotiation and binding commitment is undefined.
01 / ARCHITECTURE

Alternative architectures

01

Verifiable agent representation

Bind agent identity to a bounded organizational mandate and expiry.

02

Policy-mediated exchange

Share only task-required claims and evidence under agreed policies.

03

Human-confirmed execution

Allow automated preparation while people approve obligations at defined thresholds.

Questions for the audit

  • Which commitments may an agent propose and which may it execute?
  • How are counterpart identities, authority and data rights checked?
  • Can each company independently audit the same transaction history?

Evidence to measure

  • Actions carrying verified authorization
  • Disclosure incidents per exchange
  • End-to-end agreed transaction trace coverage
02 / DISCOVERY

Proposed first deliverable

A bounded inter-company agent protocol and audited negotiation pilot.
