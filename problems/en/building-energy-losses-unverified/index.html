RESEARCH DOSSIER · REQUIRES VALIDATION

Energy losses are detected but savings are never verified

A thermal image is not a measured financial result.

Operators may have thermographic reports, utility bills and maintenance orders but no reliable way to link observed anomalies to remedial work and measured reduction in loss. A hot component is not automatically proof of a specific defect, and repair claims need qualified inspection, safety controls and controlled comparison.

Symptoms

  • Inspections produce many heat maps with no prioritized repair plan.
  • Repair orders close without comparable after-measurements.
  • Management sees estimated savings but cannot separate actual improvement from seasonal effects.

Potential root causes

  • Diagnosis, maintenance and verification use disconnected work orders.
  • Readings lack comparable load, weather or operating conditions.
  • Work is ranked by visual anomaly rather than verified risk and savings opportunity.
01 / ARCHITECTURE

Alternative architectures

01

Measurement-to-action registry

Tie each finding to asset identity, suspected mechanism and authorized work order.

02

Qualified repair and reinspection

Dispatch trained specialists and repeat measurements in comparable conditions.

03

Savings verification architecture

Use appropriate baselines and uncertainty ranges to calculate net change.

Questions for the audit

  • What operating conditions accompanied the initial anomaly?
  • Who can safely qualify and repair the affected asset?
  • What baseline separates repair impact from changing demand or weather?

Evidence to measure

  • Findings closed with verified measurements
  • Net energy reduction against adjusted baseline
  • Cost per validated intervention
02 / DISCOVERY

Proposed first deliverable

A traceable inspection-to-repair protocol with baseline and verification plan.
