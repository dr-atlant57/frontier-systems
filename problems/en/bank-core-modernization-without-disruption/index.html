RESEARCH DOSSIER · REQUIRES VALIDATION

Modernizing a bank core without disrupting critical operations

Modernization fails when replacing technology is mistaken for preserving banking capability.

A bank may need faster product launches and AI-ready data, yet core systems encode decades of settlement rules, exceptions and regulatory controls. A big-bang replacement threatens service continuity; indefinite patching creates hidden cost and dependency. The architecture question is which business capabilities can be separated and verified without compromising the system of record.

Symptoms

  • New banking products require repeated changes to tightly coupled core code.
  • Teams maintain parallel spreadsheets because integration contracts are unclear.
  • A migration plan lists applications, but not the business outcomes that must stay correct.

Potential root causes

  • Business logic, customer state and product rules are embedded in proprietary code.
  • Testing proves individual API responses but not complete business consistency.
  • Rollback and dual-run reconciliation have not been designed into the transition.
01 / ARCHITECTURE

Alternative architectures

01

Capability-by-capability decoupling

Expose a narrow domain through stable contracts while keeping the existing system authoritative.

02

Shadow-mode validation

Run proposed calculations in parallel and compare them against authorized real cases without posting transactions.

03

Staged core transition

Move one bounded product or service after reconciliation and documented recovery rehearsal.

Questions for the audit

  • Which banking capability has the highest modernization cost today?
  • What evidence proves equivalence for settlement, balances and exceptions?
  • Who authorizes fallback when the new component disagrees with the existing core?

Evidence to measure

  • Mismatch rate on representative dual-run cases
  • Time to ship one compliant product change
  • Service disruption and recovery time in controlled tests
02 / DISCOVERY

Proposed first deliverable

A domain boundary map and reversible pilot with acceptance and reconciliation tests.
